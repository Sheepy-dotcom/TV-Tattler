Some soap characters are defined by drama; Dot Cotton was defined by decency — a
launderette matriarch whose faith, gossip and forgiveness anchored EastEnders for
a generation. She was played throughout by **June Brown**, from 1985 until 2020.

Dot's world was built from small, human things: the launderette, her cigarettes,
her Bible and an almost bottomless capacity to forgive — not least her villainous
son, Nick. It made her the show's moral conscience, the person other residents
turned to when things fell apart.

June Brown's performance was celebrated well beyond the soap. Her 2008 near-
monologue episode, in which Dot records a tape for her husband Jim, is remembered
as one of British soap's finest half-hours and earned Brown a BAFTA nomination —
a rare honour for a continuing drama.

Dot's [reference page](/characters/dot-cotton/) records both of June Brown's stints
and the gap between them.
