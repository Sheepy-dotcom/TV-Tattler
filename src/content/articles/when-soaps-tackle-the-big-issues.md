The continuing drama has a reach almost nothing else on television can match: the
same characters, in millions of living rooms, several nights a week, for years.
That reach is why British soaps have long taken on the subjects other programmes
shy away from — and, at their best, done it with real care.

Coronation Street's **Nina Lucas** (Mollie Gallagher) stood at the centre of one
of the most powerful examples in recent memory, a story about prejudice and
violence that drew on real events and prompted a national conversation. Emmerdale's
**Aaron Dingle** (Danny Miller) has been the vehicle for years of acclaimed
storytelling — on coming out, on self-harm, on mental health — praised by charities
for the honesty of its portrayal. And Coronation Street's **Paul Foreman**
(Peter Ash) brought viewers a frank, unflinching account of living with motor
neurone disease.

What unites these stories is that they never stop being about people. The issue is
never a lecture; it's a character the audience already loves, going through
something that helps others feel less alone. Done well — researched, and made with
the relevant charities — it's the most valuable thing the genre does.
