The continuing drama is one of British television's most durable inventions.
Coronation Street has run since 1960, EastEnders since 1985, Emmerdale since 1972
and Hollyoaks since 1995 — four programmes that between them have logged an
extraordinary span of the nation's viewing habits.

Their secret is continuity: characters who age with the audience, families whose
histories accumulate weight, and a rhythm of everyday life punctuated by the
occasional spectacular. A soap is never really about one story; it's about the
promise that there will always be another.
